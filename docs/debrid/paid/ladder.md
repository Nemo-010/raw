# The paid ladder

Every other document answers what a service does. This one answers what a byte
costs, on one axis, so a reader can compare a flat month, a traffic pack and a
per-file purchase without converting anything by hand.

## The answer

| question | answer |
| --- | --- |
| cheapest one TiB month | USD 3.00, TorBox Essential |
| cheapest per TiB at capacity | USD 0.154, Xunlei SVIP, third-party price |
| cheapest way to hold 10 TiB | EUR 108/year, USD 10.10/month, 1fichier Premium GOLD |
| does a free tier beat the first | no: UsersDrive's 73 GB/day is 1.99 TiB a month, but only on files UsersDrive already holds |
| does it beat the second | no: nothing free clears 4.55 TiB without an institutional identity |

⚠ **A plan is priced for its ceiling, so the two questions rank differently.** The
cheapest month is not the cheapest TiB. Xunlei's CNY 298 for 30 months with 10 TB a
month is USD 0.154/TiB, but both its price and its quota are third-party and its
vendor page returns 403, so it heads no table.

⚠ **The free tier is not a substitute for the paid month.** UsersDrive's registered
free account publishes 73 GB a day, which is 2,190 GB or 1.99 TiB per 30 days at
USD 0. That is twice the volume the working set needs, and it is spendable only on
links
to files UsersDrive already holds, on a site that says "Can I search for files other
people uploaded? No". TorBox Essential's USD 3 carries 9.095 TiB across many hosts,
so free wins on cash for a byte and loses on every other axis.

⭐ **TorBox is the only vendor that publishes an abuse floor as a promise**: 10, 20

an account. It is not an allowance, but it is stronger than "unlimited", and it is
why three TorBox tiers occupy ranks two to four of the per-TiB ladder.

## The TB and TiB trap

⛔ **Blomp, PikPak and Drime all look cheaper for 10 TB and all sell 10 decimal TB,
which is 9.09 TiB: 9% short.** Only 1fichier's unlimited hot storage, two Blomp
accounts at USD 19.98 and Hetzner BX41 at USD 45.54 actually hold 10 TiB.

The same defect has appeared on `usersdrive`, `uploadhaven` and `filefactory`: a
per-TiB figure invented for a plan whose own text says "Unlimited", which made a
flat month read up to 17 times too expensive and pushed a reader toward a smaller,
harder cap.

Two unit corrections:

- **Premiumize's 30 points a day is 900 GB, not 1,000**, and a vendor TB is not a
 TiB, so its sustained cost moves from USD 12.32 to USD 13.70.
- **DropGalaxy's 365-day price was 27% high**, USD 20.60 against USD 15.06.

## How to compare

1. Convert the offer to a monthly ceiling, and say which conversion was used:
 73 GB/day is 2.19 TB/month.
2. Convert the unit the vendor sells, not the one that reads better. A 10 TB plan is
 9.09 TiB.
3. Divide by the **TiB** the working set needs, not by the TB printed on the page.
4. For a plan with no allowance, keep it in the flat-month group. A dash is a
 finding; an invented allowance is a defect.

⚠ **No figure in this document is measured.** Every number is a vendor page or a
comparison, and the Xunlei row is third-party at both ends.
