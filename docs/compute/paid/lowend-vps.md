# The low-end VPS market: 114 rows from one tracker, 113 hosts

This page answers where a hundred cheap hosts are. `vpspricetracker.com` listed 128
providers on 2026-10-22, of which **106 had no record at all**. The topic holds
**1,411 records** and the table below has 114 rows over **113 hosts**, because `littlecreek`
and `little-creek-hosting` are one provider under two slugs.

The tracker describes itself as indexing "nearly a hundred providers" and "circa 300,000
server configurations". Its provider index listed 128 on the day it was read. Re-read on
2026-10-24, the index lists **133** and the front page says "over 250,000 VPSs from nearly a
hundred providers" over "circa 300,000 server configurations", so the tracker's own two counts
disagree and both have moved. **Twenty-one of the 133 have no row in either table here**, and
seven of those have no compute record anywhere in the corpus: DeinServerHost, Fiberstate,
GorillaServers, Hostika, NovaCloud, RoyaleHosting and NetDynamics. The other fourteen have a
record under another document. So the coverage is incomplete in both
directions: not all 128 were covered, and 114 is not the whole index.

⚠ **The tracker is a third party for every row it publishes.** It states prices it collected,
not prices it sells. So a row taken from it is `reported`. Where the vendor's own page was
also read, the vendor's figure is the one recorded and the rung is `documented`. That split is
**81 documented and 33 reported** out of 114.

⚠ **The tracker does carry a date and a stock flag per offer.** Every `/info/` page
carries an "In stock now" row, and the price-history
block dates itself: "The latest prices, collected on October 23, were: monthly: 90.00". The
search facets include `in_stock=true`. So a tracker row is dated and a stockout is visible on
the tracker as well as on the vendor page. What the tracker does not carry is a **renewal**
figure, and that absence is the one that matters most here.

## What the breadth did and did not change

**It did not move the floor.** `paid/micro.md` still holds it: USD 1.42 a month for a working
machine on an annual term, USD 3.75 for one worth a real job. The band below USD 1 belongs to
[`under-one.md`](under-one.md), and the two cheapest invoices in this table are already priced
there: `c-servers` at USD 2.39 for six months, USD 0.40 a month, and `gullo-s-hosting` at
USD 3.50 a year, USD 0.29 a month. Both are NAT, and `under-one.md` is where the NAT question is
answered rather than here: a forwarded port block carries a WireGuard listener, and does not
carry an inbound HTTP service without a relay. Breadth changed how many doors exist, not how
cheap the cheapest one is.

**It did change the shape of the market, and it reversed one of this page's own claims.** 110 of
the 114 are `paid-only`; two are `qualifies-with-caveat` (`c-servers`, `hostbrr`) and two are
`trial-only` (`clouding-io`, `tothost`). **Nineteen rows come in under USD 2 a month** once the
printed price is divided by its term, EUR at 1.1193 and GBP at 0.74804 USD, the rates dated
2026-10-23.

So the finding runs the other way: **cheap IPv4 exists under USD 2**, and the cheapest of it is
`dedirock` at USD 10.88 a year for 1 vCPU, 1 GiB and 10 GB with one dedicated IPv4 and every port
open. What the cheap rows share is a prepay rather than an address shortage: **fifteen of the
nineteen price only a six-month or longer term**, and `digirdp` prints "Semi-Annual/Annual billing
only" on a card whose headline is monthly. Only `chunkserve` (EUR 1.30), `cloudnium` (USD 1.50),
`skhron` (EUR 1.69) and `bagevm` (USD 1.99) are month to month at that price.

**It did change what the tracker can be trusted for.** The trackers are good for names and bad
for numbers, and the next section is the evidence.

## What the tracker gets wrong

Each tracker row was compared against the vendor's own page wherever the vendor answered.
Six failure modes recurred, and a reader who budgets from a tracker will meet all six.

**A cheaper plan that does not exist.** QDE's tracker page lists one plan at EUR 25.95 as its
cheapest; the vendor sells five from EUR 3.95. Linveo's tracker row is USD 3.85 for 4 GB, and
the vendor's pages list no 4 GB plan at all. HostNamaste's USD 2.00 row is not on the vendor
page, which says "Plans starting at $3.49/Month".

**A costlier plan that the vendor undercuts.** TNAHosting sells USD 16 a year that the tracker
does not list, against a tracked USD 24. HostEons sells USD 24 a year for a shape the tracker
carries at USD 17.99, whose stock is empty.

**A plan that is sold out.** ExtraVM's USD 4.50 row is marked "Sold Out" on the vendor's own
table, and the cheapest orderable plan is USD 8.00. IncogNET's 512 MB row is "Out of stock in
all locations". Neither vendor is dead, and neither row is a price that can be paid.

**Unlimited against a number.** Torchbyte's tracker row says 50,000 GB a month; the vendor says
"no traffic caps and no bandwidth billing". Ultahost's tracker row says 51,200 GB; the vendor
says "Unmetered". A tracker fills an empty allowance field with a number, and that number is
sometimes the port's line rate over 30 days rather than a term anybody published.

**A price that is two prices.** Euronodes prints EUR 4.54 for the IPv6-only shape and EUR 5.24
with an IPv4 address, and the tracker merges them. Ultahost shows 9.19, 5.52 and 5.51 for one
plan on one page.

**VAT folded into a monthly figure.** Avoro's EUR 4.07 becomes EUR 4.84 on the tracker, which
is the same number at 19 per cent VAT. The same figure appears twice independently, and
neither the tracker nor the vendor says which basis a row is on.

## What this does not establish

No machine was booted and no account was created. Every figure is a page somebody read, so the
same absence [`../gaps.md`](../gaps.md) names for the rest of the topic applies here: **four
records of the 1,411 in `dossier/compute` carry `measured`**, and this page adds none.

**Stock is a claim, not a fact.** The tracker *does* carry a per-offer stock flag: every `/info/`
page prints "In stock now", and the search facets include `in_stock=true`. So this page can now
say which rows were in stock on the day the tracker was read, and it does, in the `Stock` column.
What that column cannot establish is the stock state *today*, or whether the vendor's order
page agrees. Six rows here carry a tracker price with no working order link behind it: `extravm`,
`incognet`, `hosteons`, `kuroit`, `layer-ae` and `lowhosting`, and the table says so row by row.

**Identity is unread for 112 of the 114.** No signup page was fetched, so those records carry the
detail "not read: the signup page was not fetched in this lane" and none asserts a card
requirement. The two exceptions are `c-servers` and `hostbrr`, whose records name a card at
order. That is the single largest gap on this page: a USD 1.50 IPv6-only plan is worth nothing to
a reader if signup needs a document the reader does not hold, and 112 rows were not checked.

**The prices rot fast.** A tracker row carries its own collection date: the price-history
block reads "The latest prices, collected on October 23". It still does not carry a renewal
figure for any row. Treat every figure here as of 2026-10-22 unless the row says otherwise,
and re-read the vendor before acting.
