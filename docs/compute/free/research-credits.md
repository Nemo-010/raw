# Research credits: which programmes an unaffiliated researcher can enter

Twenty-eight programmes in `dossier/compute/` call themselves a research credit, an academic
grant, an open-source sponsorship or an independent-researcher grant. This document answers one
question: which can a person with no institution, no company and no academic email enter?

The two largest numbers in the slice are unreachable. The Swiss AI Initiative aims to distribute
10-20 million GPU hours across 2026 (`swiss-ai.org/compute-grants`, read 2026-10-22). NVIDIA offers
up to 30,000 H100 80 GB hours, and is also not accepting applications
(`nvidia.com/en-us/industries/higher-education-research/academic-grant-program/`, read 2026-10-22).

⭐ A programme that asks for an "official academic
email" or an "institutional email" is closed permanently. The check runs on the address before a
proposal is read, and no proposal changes a domain.

The count is 15 shut and 13 open, and two of the open ones have a shut window today. The published
credits that need no institution total USD 6,500 across three vendors.

## Gate A: an individual with a project description

Nine programmes ask for a written description of the work and nothing else, which makes this the one
gate in the class a reader can pass tonight.

`open-intelligence-foundation` takes an eight-field form and none is institutional, and its
use-of-funds list includes "Living Expenses" beside GPUs
(`openintelligencefoundation.ai/request-funding.html`, read 2026-10-22). The ceiling is "Up to
$10,000 per grant", and the failure is supply: one named sponsor, one award in eight months.

`compound-reverie-grants` pays "as little as $500 to as much as $7,500 each, in fully non-dilutive,
no-strings-attached capital", and names "thoughtful people who are simply in transition"
(`compound.vc/grants`, read 2026-10-22). `lambda-research-grant` publishes a bigger number and no
rule at all: "qualifying researchers up to $5,000 in cloud credits" for "independent researchers who
need compute" (`lambda.ai/blog/iclr-2026-12-papers`, read 2026-06-05).

`yotta-labs-academic` and `fal-research-grants` name the door in words. Yotta lists "Other
Independent Researchers - Anyone conducting their own research projects" for "$1000 Credits", and
all three of its sponsorships are university labs, so the lane is published and not demonstrated
(`yottalabs.ai/research-credit`, read 2026-10-22). fal says "No formal degree is required" and
publishes no amount (`fal.ai/grants`, read 2026-10-22). `miracleplus-compute` names independent
researchers globally, "Your physical location or business registration does not affect eligibility"
(`miracleplus.com/compute/`, read 2026-10-22), but its intake reuses the accelerator questionnaire.

`emergent-ventures` is the widest door with the least published money. The only stated bar is age,
"all applicants must be 13 years of age or older", and on the amount "There is NO typical grant range
or average award amount" (`mercatus.tfaforms.net/5099527`, read 2026-10-22). One condition decides the
work: it "no longer accepts AI-written applications ... and reject those using AI for anything other
than proofreading". ⚠ The "USD 1k to USD 50k typical" figure comes from third-party
trackers, not from an operator page.

`o-shaughnessy-fellowship` and `the-eigenprize` are the two largest awards here and both windows are
shut. O'Shaughnessy pays up to USD 100,000 equity-free to "adults worldwide, regardless of their
background", covers living costs, and states "no deadline extensions" on a window that closed on 30
April 2026 (`help.osv.llc/article/10-faqs`, read 2026-10-22). The Eigenprize paid USD 250,000 to 23
named people, "We take no equity and have zero expectations" (`eigenprize.build/`, read 2026-10-22),
and shut on 31 March 2026.

⭐ Both are worth a reminder and neither is worth an application today. A reader who starts from the
winners list spends the session on a page that reads "The winners have been selected."

## Gate B: an academic email address

`aws-cloud-credit` states the rule hardest: "We require applicants provide their official
institution-issued email ID (.edu, .org, or equivalent) ... Applications from non-institution
domains will not be accepted"
(`aws.amazon.com/government-education/research-and-technical-computing/cloud-credit-for-research/faqs/`,
read 2026-10-22). The amount is why a reader chases it: "Student awards will be up to a maximum of
$5,000.00. Faculty and Staff awards are not capped." The role list is three entries, all
institutional, and review takes 90 to 120 days.

`amd-university-program` states the rule three times across three pages: "Membership is open
exclusively to faculty and researchers at universities and research institutions worldwide. Enroll
using your official academic email address - personal emails won't qualify"
(`amd.com/en/corporate/university-program/enroll.html`, read 2026-10-22). The award is node-hours on
a Slurm cluster over SSH. The tooling is ordinary. The door is not.

`google-cloud-research` and `anthropic-ai` close on the same condition by different means. Google
requires `Organization Name *` chosen from "the provided list" plus a faculty-directory profile
link, and pays USD 1,000 a year to a PhD student (`edu.google.com/programs/credits/research/`, read
2026-10-22). Anthropic offers "up to $20,000 in API credits" to researchers "attached to research
institutions", evaluated "on the first Monday of each month"
(`support.claude.com/en/articles/11199177-anthropic-s-ai-for-science-program`, read 2026-10-22).

Three programmes publish both a maximum and an institution requirement: USD 20,000, USD 5,000 and
USD 5,000. The sum is USD 30,000, and a reader with no institution can apply to none of them.

## Gate C: an institution, usually with a PI

`nvidia-academic-grant` is the strongest of these six programmes: "Up to 30,000 H100 80 GB hours or
equivalent (max. of eight concurrent GPUs)", expiring "six months after the award". Eligibility is "a
full-time faculty member at an accredited academic institution that awards research degrees to PhD
students", and postdocs "must work with a full-time faculty member to submit on their behalf"
(`dam-cdn.nvd.orangelogic.com/AssetLink/drvk2c5agrv0s0myqncgl63l4f8lc550.pdf`, read 2026-10-22).
The portal is shut as well, confirmed by a staff reply on 2026-06-30.

`swiss-ai-initiative` is the largest and the most clearly closed: "10-20 million GPU hours" in 2026
on more than 10,000 GH200 GPUs, with a small project getting "typically ... between 20k and 32k
compute hours". The condition is "at least one team member is employed at a recognized research
institution in Switzerland, at a minimum 50% FTE research function ... for the whole duration", plus
"signature of at least 1 supporting Swiss PI" (`swiss-ai.org/compute-grants`, read 2026-10-22).

`nairr-pilot` is the one a reader is most likely to be told to try, and its exclusion is written
out: "open to meritorious proposals by US-based researchers and educators from US-based
institutions", and "the email address you provide must be your institutional email. Requests
providing personal email addresses such as .gmail.com addresses will not be considered"
(`nairrpilot.org/opportunities/allocations`, read 2026-10-22). One clause softens nothing that
matters: "Proposals for research activities without merit-reviewed support will be accepted" removes
a precondition inside the eligible class and creates no unaffiliated lane.

`nebius-research-grants` and `azure-research-credits` publish no ceiling at all. Nebius names four
academic roles and sets the amount "individually based on each proposal", with notification "in late
Q3 to early Q4 2026" (`nebius.com/nebius-research-grants`, read 2026-10-22). Azure's only route is a
form requiring a work email and an institution name (`microsoft.com/en-us/azure-academic-research/`,
read 2026-10-22).

`microsoft-ai` names "individual researchers" as eligible, which makes it the sharpest case of an
identity gate hiding a place gate: projects "must be based in or benefit Washington State and its
residents", out of "a total of $5 million in Azure service credits"
(`microsoft.com/en-us/research/academic-program/ai-for-good-lab-open-call/`, read 2026-10-22).

## Gate D: a company, a licence or a municipality

`xuhui-district-ai` reimburses compute a registered entity has already paid for: "this policy applies
to legally operating industrial legal persons or non-legal-person organisations", and the pack
requires "a copy of the applicant unit's business licence" (`sh-keji.cn/kjcx/26125.html`, read
2026-10-12). It presupposes the company and the cash to front.

`haidian-district-elastic` is the municipal budget behind `miracleplus-compute`: "no more than CNY
27,000,000" in year one, serving "no fewer than 30 quality projects or talent teams each year", for a
founder meeting one of six criteria such as "fine-tuning experience on 8 or more GPUs"
(`zyk.bjhd.gov.cn/zwdt/zcjd/202501/t20250121_4755086.shtml`, read 2026-10-22). CNY 27,000,000
across 30 projects is CNY 900,000 an award. The million is a per-project cap inside a fixed budget
line, not a standard award.

⚠ Do not merge the two. The accelerator allocates its own credits to individual researchers, and the
Xuhui instrument reimburses corporate spend.

## Gate E: an open-source contribution with traction

`dartnode-open-source` is the largest shape and the hardest number: "Eligible projects have a public
GitHub repository with 1,000+ stars that is at least 90 days old (no forks or archived repos)",
checked automatically once a badge is in the README (`dartnode.com/open-source`, read 2026-10-22).
The box is 1 vCPU, 1 GiB and 10 GB SSD, and the paid ladder prices the same shape at "VPS-1
$3.95/mo", which makes it worth USD 47.40 a year.

`zenix-open-source-sponsorship` asks for less reputation and gives an unknown amount: an OSI licence,
"At least six months old", activity in 90 days, "two-or-more" maintainers, and one signal from "100
GitHub stars, 1,000 monthly downloads, three outside contributors, or 100 active members"
(`zenix.sg/open-source`, read 2026-10-22). Its cohort table reads "0 of 10 seats filled".

`zylora-open-source` publishes a conversion and the conversion does not hold: the credit is USD 500,
the project needs "a meaningful GitHub presence (200+ stars)", and the table says USD 500 buys "H100
SXM 4.6h at USD 3.49/hr" (`zylora.dev/free-gpu-credits/`, read 2026-10-22). See below.

`atlas-cloud-open` is the only recurring credit here, recorded at `reported` because the vendor page
redirected to the homepage: Starter at USD 50 to 150 a month, Growth at USD 150 to 400, Flagship at
USD 400 to 1,500, per two third parties
(`iterm.today/en/offer/atlas-cloud-open-source-program/`, read 2026-10-22). `webdock` is `reported`
for the same reason plus one LinkedIn post and a company identity unresolved between the post handle
and the programme name. ⚠ Neither should be planned against.

## Converting a credit to hours

Only three records carry both a dollar figure and an hourly rate, so the class produces three
divisions and no total.

`google-tpu-research` checks out at both ends: the vendor's "USD 2.20" per v3 chip-hour
(`cloud.google.com/tpu/pricing/`, read 2026-10-22), and an activation email granting "5 on-demand
Cloud TPU v3-8 device(s)", which is 40 chips.

```text
USD 126,720 / USD 2.20 per chip-hour = 57,600 chip-hours
57,600 chip-hours / 40 chips         = 1,440 hours
1,440 hours / 24 hours a day         = 60 days
```

All three lines agree with the record's 60-day figure. The grant also carries 40 on-demand v2 chips
and 800 preemptible v2 chips, left out of the price.

`google-cloud-research` converts to roughly 800 TPU v5e chip-hours, which is weaker than it looks:
the record divides USD 1,000 by USD 1.20, but no evidence row carries the USD 1.20 rate, and the USD
1,000 comes from a Wayback capture of 2026-03-17. Treat 800 as the record's estimate.

`zylora-open-source` does not convert. At the rate on the same page:

```text
USD 500 / USD 3.49 per H100-hour = 143 H100-hours
vendor page states                 4.6 H100-hours
```

The two differ by a factor of about 31, so one describes a different credit size than USD 500. The
record repeats the vendor's 4.6 hours without running the division and inherits the contradiction.

`nvidia-academic-grant` needs no rate because the award is already in hours. Eight GPUs running
continuously consume 30,000 hours in 3,750 hours of wall-clock time, which is 156 days. The expiry is
six months, about 183 days, so a full-time eight-GPU run fits with 27 days to spare, and anything
less than full-time use leaves the grant unspent.

`swiss-ai-initiative` gives 20,000 to 32,000 hours for a small project and starts the large call at
"more than 500k GPU hours", 15.6 times the small ceiling. The record never says whether a compute hour
is a chip-hour or a node-hour, so no chip count follows from it. `miracleplus-compute` fixes the shape
instead: 8 H800 cards for 15 months is 120 card-months, so CNY 1,000,000 is CNY 8,333 a card-month.

The three reachable vendor credits with a published ceiling are USD 5,000, USD 1,000 and USD 500.
That totals USD 6,500 and stays in dollars, because two of the three records carry no hourly rate.

## The refutations

Three programmes are cited so often that a reader arrives believing they are open.

`aws-cloud-credit` is the most useful negative here. It publishes USD 5,000 for a student and an
uncapped faculty award, then fails twice: the domain rule is absolute, and the role list has no
fourth entry. `nairr-pilot` publishes no amount at all, so there is no number to check, only a
prestige, and it names the .gmail case explicitly.

`prime-intellect-2` is gone. Two figures circulate, "USD 500 to USD 100,000 Fast Compute Grants" and
"USD 50 free compute at ICLR". Both pages returned "HTTP/1.1 404 Not Found" on 2026-10-22, and the
ICLR page carried an expiry of 31 December 2024. There is no signup credit either
(`docs.primeintellect.ai/faq`, read 2026-10-22).

⭐ The class holds the reverse error too, and it costs more. `google-tpu-research` is assumed to
require an academic affiliation. It does not: "People sometimes assume that e.g. an academic
affiliation is required to participate, but that isn't true; we want the program to be as open as
possible" (`news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36937636`, read 2026-10-22). The door there is a card for
billing verification, and a reader who skips it on the name loses the largest unaffiliated
allocation in the class.

## What a rejection costs

The cadence decides whether a refusal is a delay or a year, and it does not track the size of the
award.

| Cadence | Programmes | What a refusal costs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a working week or less | `open-intelligence-foundation`, `compound-reverie-grants`, `emergent-ventures` | a rewrite, then a resubmission |
| monthly or rolling | `anthropic-ai`, `lambda-research-grant`, `fal-research-grants`, `google-tpu-research`, `yotta-labs-academic` | one cycle, then the same application |
| a quarter or more | `amd-university-program`, `aws-cloud-credit`, `google-cloud-research`, `nvidia-academic-grant`, `zenix-open-source-sponsorship` | a season |
| annual, fixed window | `nebius-research-grants`, `o-shaughnessy-fellowship`, `the-eigenprize` | a year, on possibly different terms |
| no route | `prime-intellect-2`, `xuhui-district-ai` | nothing to retry |

The two largest reachable cash awards, USD 100,000 from `o-shaughnessy-fellowship` and USD 100,000
from `the-eigenprize`, are the two with a shut window. The fastest answers come with the smallest
ceilings. And the quarter-long reviews belong to programmes that reject on a domain before reading a
proposal, so the wait measures the queue and not the merit.

Two rules are stated rather than implied. `o-shaughnessy-fellowship` publishes "There will be no
deadline extensions", and `miracleplus-compute` publishes that "an applicant not admitted may reapply
in a later round". ⚠ No record here carries an appeal route, and only one publishes an acceptance
rate: `o-shaughnessy-fellowship` reports 24 fellows from 11,813 applications, 0.20 percent, and the
roughly 20 grants in the sister lane lift it to about 0.37 percent.

## What the class does not give

It does not give national high-performance computing. Every national allocation here is bound to an
employment contract or a PI: Swiss at 50% FTE, NAIRR at a US institution, NVIDIA at a faculty post.

It does not give a route past a closed cycle, or a durable recurring credit. `the-eigenprize` reads
"The winners have been selected" with no next-round date, and `atlas-cloud-open` renews at the
vendor's discretion.

It does not give an amount knowable before the decision. Five records publish no number at all:
`azure-research-credits`, `nebius-research-grants`, `nairr-pilot`, `fal-research-grants` and
`emergent-ventures`.

## How it composes

Cash composes with everything, because the recipient chooses the vendor:
`open-intelligence-foundation` lists living costs and hardware beside compute, and
`o-shaughnessy-fellowship` covers "reasonable living and other expenses".

Credits compose only with their own vendor. The exception is `google-tpu-research`, whose quota
attaches to the applicant's own Google Cloud project, so the accelerator is free and the host VM is
billed at list. `dartnode-open-source` composes as infrastructure instead: a routable IPv4 without
NAT makes it an origin for a fronted service rather than a jump host.

## What would change the answer

A first-party grants page at `zylora.dev` that prints the credit size above its hours table would
resolve the factor-of-31 contradiction and settle whether the award is 4.6 or 143 H100-hours. A
current TRC activation email would settle the quota a new grant is set to in 2026.

A live Lambda terms page would settle whether expiry exists and whether an affiliation is required,
which is why the record stays at `documented`. A reopening date for `the-eigenprize` or
`o-shaughnessy-fellowship` would turn two dormant rows into the largest reachable awards here.
